Product guide for Circuit League

A pickleball league that organizes itself.

Circuit League is an automated matchmaking league inside the Circuit mobile app. A player says when they can play and where. Circuit finds opponents of the right level, proposes one concrete match, and handles scoring, ratings, leaderboards and DUPR reporting afterward. There is no organizer, no schedule to follow and no weekly commitment.

This guide explains how every part works, in plain language, with mockups of the main screens. It is written for product review. Everything here comes from the owner-approved product brief.

How to read the labels. Launch ships when the League opens in Austin. After launch is decided but built later. Deferred is postponed to a later season or phase. Not planned is explicitly out of scope.

Screens shown are illustrative mockups of intent. Exact layouts and copy are settled in design.

Overview

What the League is, in one page

For players

Tell Circuit the times and venues that work. Get one proposed match at a time. Accept, tweak it or pass. Play, report the score, and watch your local position move.

For venues

More regulars playing at their courts. Later, League Nights fill off-peak hours. Players pay the venue directly; Circuit never touches court money.

For Circuit

A new player-facing product that runs on automation, with the owner reviewing a single queue. No revenue model at launch.

The big ideas

  1. Circuit picks the match. Players answer one proposal instead of browsing a list or negotiating in an open-ended chat.
  2. Standings come from results, not DUPR. Circuit keeps its own private rating, seeded once from DUPR. The number is never shown. Players see rank, movement and record.
  3. Every game counts and goes to DUPR. Confirmed League results are sent to DUPR as League matches.
  4. No fixed divisions or tiers. Matching uses rating closeness, with a fairness limit.
  5. Mobile only. League play lives in the mobile app. The website introduces the League and hosts a few supporting pages, never gameplay.
  6. Circuit never handles court money. No booking, no deposits, no fees collected.

Finding opponents

How matchmaking works

A matching batch runs every 15 minutes, plus a quick run within 2 minutes of a change. It looks for a group that fits everyone's times, shares a venue everyone marked Preferred or OK, and is close enough in level.

What a search is

  • Each format (singles, rotating doubles) has its own search, and each fixed team has its own. A player can run several at once.
  • A confirmed match stops only the search that found it, and drops the clashing times from the player's other searches.
  • There is no weekly cap on matches. A Tuesday match does not block a Sunday one.
  • Skip this week and Automatic weekly matchmaking control the weekly restart. Stopping never cancels a confirmed match.
  • Next week's times can be entered early. They are matched once inside the 7-day window.
  • If someone else cancels a confirmed match, the others go back into search automatically.

Fairness limit

Circuit only proposes matches inside a fairness limit, judged on the worst pairing in the match (for rotating doubles, the worst rotation). It relaxes the longer a search has waited, and never goes past 70/30 automatically.

Fixed teams are judged on the two Team ratings, so an uneven pair such as 3.8 and 3.1 is matched on team strength. Automatic widening is on by default and a player can turn it off. The search clock starts when a search starts and resets only after a confirmed match from that search; editing times or declining does not reset it.

Widening when no match appears

0 – 48 hoursOnly the player's saved times and venues, inside the fairness limit for that search age.
After 48 hoursCircuit offers other times (even well outside saved windows, such as 7 PM for someone who saved 4–5 PM) and one extra venue. These are labelled Outside your usual times. Nothing changes until the player approves.
After 72 hoursCircuit can offer a Mixed match to a Men's or Women's doubles searcher (or the reverse), again only with approval. Singles has no Mixed. Doubles players can also pick several categories at setup, such as Women's and Mixed.

Approval is always explicit. A widening option never turns into a match by itself. Auto-accept works only inside a player's saved times and saved venues, never for an "Outside your usual times" alternative.

When pools are thin

The League needs enough searchers to work. A simulation of Cedar Park schedules matched about 30% of searchers within 7 days at 75 searchers, 55% at 150 and 72% at 300. The launch now spans the whole Austin area, so members are spread over more venues than that simulation assumed, and since a match needs a venue everyone chose, real rates may be lower. The thinnest pools at launch are expected to be Women's singles, fixed Women's doubles and fixed Mixed.

The core moment

Match found

When Circuit finds a group, everyone receives the same proposal at once: a time, a venue, a number of games and points. It is one decision, not a list to compare.

What a player can do

  • Accept. When everyone accepts the same plan, the match is confirmed.
  • Choose another time or venue. Up to 3 alternatives, some labelled "Outside your usual times". The player's own acceptance carries over to the plan they choose. The latest counter replaces any earlier one and does not extend the hold; after 3 rounds without agreement the proposal closes as "Not this time".
  • Not this time. Everyone returns to search. No penalty. Circuit does not re-offer the same group under unchanged conditions for 7 days.
  • Hide this group. Declines and hides that group for 7 days. After 3 hides in a row Circuit suggests reviewing search settings.
  • Chat. A match chat opens with the proposal for logistics ("where do we park?"). Agreeing a change in chat does not change the plan; someone has to send it as "Choose another time" and everyone must accept.

Match labels

The start is the best half-hour time inside everyone's saved windows, between 6 hours and 7 days ahead. Each proposal shows how even the match is: Close match Slight favourite Stretch match. No numbers are shown.

After the handshake

Confirmed match, play, and scoring

Before the match

  • Reminders: 24 hours before (only if confirmed more than 24 hours ahead) and 1 hour before. They ask for no response.
  • Calendar: add the match to a phone calendar.
  • Paid venues: every match is labelled Free or Paid. On a paid venue a "Who's booking?" line lets one player tap "I'll book the court" so everyone knows who is reserving it; Circuit books nothing and never claims a court is available. A venue without lights is never proposed for a match that would end after sunset.
  • Optional check-in: if both players allow location around match time, a check-in lowers the result's review risk. It is evidence that phones were nearby, not proof the match was played, and other players see only the venue name. Players who decline can still play.
  • Circuit does not reserve courts. A suggested court is not a booking. Players get access and pay the venue themselves.
  • Cancelling: a player who cannot make it cancels. Everyone is told who cancelled and the rest rejoin matchmaking. Cancelling within 12 hours of the start is a late cancellation (see Safety for what that costs). For rain, a player cancels and everyone goes back into matchmaking (at most a warning for the canceller).
  • Scan to play matches start already Confirmed, because everyone is at the venue.

Scoring rules

Every game is played

Not best-of, so every game is rated. Games go to 11 or 15, win by 2. Singles and fixed doubles play 3 or 5 games. Rotating doubles plays 3 (each partner pairing once) or 6; rotating Mixed has two valid pairings, so 4 games. The format is set in the proposal and visible before play.

One reports, one confirms

One player reports; someone on the opposing side confirms. Silence auto-confirms after 24 hours. Nobody can confirm their own report.

48-hour deadline

If nobody reports within 48 hours of the start, the match ends as "No result". No rating change, no fabricated score. Reminders go to every player by push and email: 1 hour after the planned end, again at 24 hours, and a final warning 6 hours before the deadline. A no-show can still be reported inside the window.

Reading results

Everywhere a result appears, the winner is listed first, teams are stacked vertically with each score attached to its team, and a colored accent bar and text weight show winner (green) and loser (coral). Circuit never shows a single "A vs B" line with a detached score.

Doubles with rotating partners

Partners become opponents in other games, so each game settles on its own and is confirmed by someone who played against the reporter in that game. Real per-game participants are always preserved.

Adding games on the spot

After a match, players can add extra games (capped at 9 added games per match). They rate and send to DUPR like any other game.

Disputes and corrections

Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram.

  • While disputed, the result stays out of the rating. If players cannot agree in 24 hours, it is voided. A voided result is deleted from DUPR.
  • In doubles, the first answer from the opposing side settles it: once either opponent confirms, the result stands and a later dispute from their partner does not count. Both sides reporting different scores opens a dispute automatically, and a dispute needs a reason.
  • A confirmed result can be corrected for 7 days through a "Problem with this result" form. A correction replays later ratings, which can move players who were not in the match.

Stopped early (injury)

If a game ends early, for example an injured player, the score at that moment is kept and the side that stopped counts as losing that game. Nothing is invented: there is no "11-0".

Example. Ben rolls his ankle trailing 4–7 in game 2. Ana enters game 1 (11–8) and 4–7 for game 2, and marks Ben's side as stopping. Game 1 counts as played, game 2 is a loss for Ben's side, and Ben's side loses the match.

This applies everywhere in Circuit (League, tournaments, quick tournaments and ladders). For DUPR, a stopped game goes as a normal rated game only when the side that kept playing led with at least 6 points; otherwise it is not sent.

DUPR reporting

  • Every confirmed result is sent to DUPR, one DUPR match per game, in every format, as a League match (DUPR play type LEAGUE, which DUPR accepts from every partner; organizer ladders send the same). It uses the same pipeline organizer ladders use (retries, receipts), and every player links DUPR before playing.
  • Circuit checks DUPR access when a player joins. The owner can retry failed sends one at a time or in bulk and pause sends during a DUPR outage.
  • A voided result is deleted from DUPR.
  • Circuit never claims a DUPR change it has not received. The app distinguishes pending, failed and synchronized.
  • Circuit's rating never changes anyone's DUPR rating, and DUPR never orders League standings.
Fair matching and standings

The Circuit Rating (private)

The Circuit Rating is a hidden skill estimate. It decides who is matched with whom and orders the boards. Players never see the number.

What players see

Rank, movement ("Up 3"), record and a one-line reason for each change. Never a number, never a rating chart.

How it starts

Seeded once from DUPR (with its reliability). No DUPR rating? Self-placement at 2.5 / 3.0 / "3.5 or higher". After that, only League results move it.

Three pools

Singles, Doubles and Teams are separate. A singles win never moves a doubles position. Category (Men's, Women's, Mixed) is a filter, not a pool.

How it moves

  • Games, not matches. Each game is a win or loss; margin of victory does not matter. All games in a match are weighted so a 5-game match does not count five times a 1-game match. The order of wins does not matter (W-W-L equals L-W-W).
  • Uncertainty. Each player has a confidence level. New players move fast; settled players move slowly. Returning after a long break speeds movement up a little.
  • Repeat opponents count less. Beating the same opponents again within 30 days earns less each time, which discourages farming wins with friends.
  • Teams. A new fixed pair starts from the average of the two partners' Doubles ratings with low confidence, then learns from its own results. A new partner means a new Team rating; old history stays with the old pair.
  • Ties in rank share the same position.
  • Reasons are stored. Every rating change is stored against the result that caused it, so Circuit can explain movement and repair corrected results.

Position and progress

Leaderboards, history, seasons and the weekly recap

Boards

  • Geographic: city → metro → state → global, using the player's declared home city (never live GPS). Example: Cedar Park → Austin area → Texas → League.
  • Venue boards: players with at least 2 confirmed League matches at that venue this season. Labelled "League players active at the venue", not "all members". Shows who plays there, never when.
  • Fixed-pair boards are separate, ordered by the Team rating.
  • A board needs at least 5 listed players to show (provisional players count); otherwise it says "Not enough players yet" with an invite prompt and shows the player's own season record. Boards refresh weekly (Sunday 11:59 PM Central snapshot) and are visible to signed-in League members in the app, not on the public website.
  • An established player is listed after their first confirmed match of the season; a provisional player after 3, marked Provisional. Once listed, a player stays listed all season. There is no rating decay, and each row shows matches played this season rather than marking anyone inactive.
  • Home city is declared and can change once every 90 days. Equal ratings share a rank (1, 1, 1, 4).
  • Ranking depends on the pool's Circuit Rating only. Wins, win rate and point differential are shown as statistics but never rank anyone.

Player history and stats

  • Current and best position, rank-over-time chart, weekly movement
  • Match history with real partners, opponents, agreed scoring format, game scores and status. Cancelled, disputed or voided entries never look like wins or losses.
  • Wins, losses, win rate, point differential, with game-versus-match units clear
  • Season and lifetime summaries; separate individual and team records
  • Matches played and "member since" on match cards

Achievement pills

Small pills on confirmed results, shared with tournaments and ladders: 11-0 pickle Double pickle Comeback win Clutch Deuce drama Revenge Rivalry. League-specific: Upset win (beat someone clearly higher rated; no number shown) and Took a game off the #1. Week pills: Perfect games Undefeated Win streak Up 3 (rose 2+ places) Season high. Rank-based pills apply to any listed player, provisional or not, but a first appearance on a board or losing the Provisional label never counts as moving up.

Pills celebrate the player only, appear for confirmed results only (never a forfeit, pending, disputed or voided match), use no red or coral, never trigger their own push or email, show at most 5 per row, are recomputed if a correction changes the result, and shareable cards never name the opponent.

The weekly recap

The recap is a week in review, not a separate weekly event. At the Sunday cutoff Circuit freezes the snapshot, refreshes boards and explains movement. On Monday at 8 AM local time players get an app screen and an email with results, movement, trends, separately labelled DUPR changes and notable activity nearby. If a player did not play, the recap says so honestly, and distinguishes their own inactivity from others passing them.

Seasons

Quarterly

Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter. The League continues indefinitely, and players can join at any point in a quarter.

Season 1 Launch

Runs from launch to the end of that quarter if at least 6 weeks remain, otherwise through the next quarter. Season-end email and screen for everyone with a confirmed match, plus badges. Season #1 goes to an established player with at least 6 matches that season on a board of at least 8; if the top player is provisional, the highest established player with 6 matches gets it. Season completion is 8 confirmed matches. Founding Member is earned in Season 1; Recruiter once 3 invited friends have played a confirmed match.

Season Finals Season 2

One bracket per division (format plus category) per area. Players opt in; at the week-8 snapshot, established entries with at least 6 matches qualify, up to 128. Bracket size is the largest of 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128 the qualifiers fill, so no byes; fewer than 8 means no bracket. With 32 or more, entries split into skill bands of at least 16, using the higher of week-8 rating and season peak minus 0.15 so nobody tanks into a weaker band. Weeks 9–13; rounds last 7 days (5 days for 64 and 128). Rotating doubles has no Finals at launch. If a round match is not played by its deadline, the side that offered availability and responded advances; if both or neither did, the higher seed advances. Not run if early health targets are missed.

Seasons are Spring (Mar–May), Summer (Jun–Aug), Fall (Sep–Nov) and Winter (Dec–Feb). Results count by the date played: the season-end snapshot is taken 7 days after the last day, after report, confirmation and dispute windows have run, so a match played on the last day still counts. The League tab shows the final standings date in the season's last two weeks. When a season ends, standings freeze and the season board keeps showing where everyone finished. The next season's boards fill as players play. Ratings, history and badges carry over. There is no separate "leave the League" action; a player steps away by stopping matchmaking.

Partners and places

My Teams and the venue directory

My Teams (fixed partners)

  • A player invites a partner by search or link; the partner accepts; they name the team and choose when it is searching.
  • Up to 3 active teams per player. A team respects both partners' times and commitments; Circuit never substitutes a partner.
  • Team setup intersects both partners' preferences without exposing either person's full calendar.
  • Renaming or recreating the same pair does not reset its rating, sanctions or history. A new partner is a new team.
  • Partner changes: if a partner is banned, blocks the teammate or deletes their account, the team ends and its upcoming matches are cancelled with no penalty to the remaining partner. The remaining partner keeps their individual Doubles rating. An injured or paused partner does not end the team; a team "Out injured" switch pauses its matching.
  • No substitutes.

Venues

  • Circuit maintains a trusted directory: stable IDs, reviewed names and map pins, address, time zone, access notes, Free or Paid, and active or closed.
  • Players can Suggest a venue or a correction. The owner reviews duplicates, closures and misleading entries before publishing.
  • "Only these venues" is a hard rule: a Match found needs a venue every player marked Preferred or OK. A nearby alternative can only appear as an approved widening.
  • A venue listing promises nothing about access, membership or an open court. Players obtain access and pay the venue directly.
  • Players can re-select venues using their current location ("Use my current location") without it becoming their home area.
  • A popular times heat map helps players pick times. Circuit never invents popularity or available courts.
  • Every Match found and alternative shows Free or Paid. A paid venue's page adds a short note ("Non-members pay per court hour; reserve ahead") and never shows prices Circuit cannot keep current.
  • Launch venues (such as Tejas Pickleball, Apex Cedar Park) are hand-entered after the owner verifies exact name, location, court count and free or paid status.
Trust and safety

Reviews, blocks, reports and sanctions

Post-match reviews

Optional, within 48 hours: thumbs up or down for on time, communication and sportsmanship, plus an optional comment. This is conduct, never skill. Feedback is private to Circuit; a player sees their own themes only after 5 reviews from 3 people. Reviews never change a rating, and there is no public conduct score.

Mute, block, report

Mute hides a player's chat messages; it is unlimited and the other player is not told. Block also stops Circuit from matching you together, capped at 5 active blocks so it cannot be used to dodge strong players; blocks never change standings, and block patterns are flagged to the owner. Blocks apply to League only. Report goes to the owner's review queue, and the owner gets a phone alert the moment a report or an intimate-image removal request arrives.

Cancellations and no-shows

Cancelling within 12 hours of the start is late. A late cancellation or no-show counts once it is reported and either uncontested or upheld. The first is a warning; three in 60 days is a 7-day matchmaking pause; repeats after that are 30 days. A reported no-show stands if the player has not challenged it within 48 hours. Rained-out late cancellations earn at most a warning. Declined or expired offers are never counted as misconduct.

Appeals

A restricted player sees the reason, the affected capability and how to appeal. Appeals are accepted within 14 days and reviewed within 3 business days. Allegations and findings are kept distinct; nobody is banned automatically from star averages or dispute counts.

Policies behind the product

  • Gender eligibility: published rule following USA Pickleball policy, applied exactly as written.
  • Photos: required, built once for all of Circuit (not League-only). Every new photo is checked on the server for nudity and hate imagery before it shows. A clean photo appears at once; a flagged or unscreenable one is held for the owner ("Your photo is being reviewed") while others see the player's initials. A reported photo is hidden at once, and the player can keep playing meanwhile. Federal 48-hour removal for intimate imagery is honored.
  • Account deletion: in the app and from a web page. The profile and photo are deleted and past opponents see "Former player". League play never blocks deletion, except a 14-day wait while the account is on the owner's review list, and deletion never stops a void or the DUPR deletion that follows it. Results already at DUPR stay there until deleted through DUPR.
  • Chat: covered by reporting, mute, block, owner message hiding and a published support contact. A word filter is added only if App Review asks.
  • Apple UGC rules: report, block and moderation tools are in place.
  • Waiver and terms: release with express-negligence wording, liability cap, Texas law, arbitration and class-action waiver. No insurance (accepted).
  • Review queue: the owner works a single queue at launch; an AI operations agent helps after launch (see Operations).

Privacy promises

  • A Match found shows only the shared time and its alternatives, never another player's calendar.
  • No precise home location, raw availability, private feedback, blocks, reports or rating numbers appear on any public read.
  • Circuit never claims to know where a player is, never shows a live 0–0 or "playing now", and does not require continuous location.
  • Names default to first name and last initial, with a "Show full name" setting.
  • Match chat closes 7 days after the settled result or cancellation, and is retained privately for 180 days for disputes and reports.

Rules of restraint

  • No quiet-hours feature; players use Do Not Disturb. Only Match found has an overnight rule (none from 10 PM – 7 AM).
  • Snooze applies only to Match found: while it lasts, no new offers are sent.
  • Caps: proposal progress at most once per proposal per 2 hours; play-again nudges at most 1 per 72 hours and 2 per 14 days; invite pushes 1 per 14 days; at most 2 non-match pushes a day; chat at most 6 pushes per thread per day.
  • Separate controls for Match found, Committed matches and results, Match chat, Weekly recap, Play-again nudges and League announcements. Account and safety notices cannot be turned off.
  • Notification groups and caps keep volume low; Match found has no extra cap because each search has at most one pending offer.

Never sent

  • Claims about where a player is
  • A live 0–0 or "playing now"
  • Rank-drop pushes
  • SMS (not at launch)
  • A push just for earning a pill

Android gets two notification channels, "Match updates" (high importance) and "League news", shipped in the same app update as the League.

Getting enough players

Growth: Scan to play, invites and recruiting

Matching quality depends on volume, so the League builds recruiting into the product from day one.

Scan to play Launch

Every League player has a permanent personal QR code. Three players at a park meet a fourth who has no account. The newcomer scans a code with the phone camera, installs the app and signs up (profile gender, age check, waiver, photo, DUPR link). The group plays rotating doubles and reports, and the match starts already Confirmed. The result is confirmed and every game goes to DUPR.

  • In-person only; it is not an instant-play queue. Each scanned player confirms on their own phone; doubles needs all four.
  • The QR scans only while it is open on its owner's phone, so a screenshot does not work.
  • If a rotating-doubles fourth player does not show, the three present report the no-show and can scan in any eligible player to keep the match.
  • Circuit checks every player is a League member with a photo, no block between them and no restriction. A large skill gap shows a warning but is allowed.
  • A recruit needs profile gender, the age check, the waiver, a photo and a linked DUPR account before playing.
  • Recruits finish the rest of setup after their first match.
  • A friend-ring farming rating through Scan to play is an accepted risk, no worse than DUPR's own.

Invites

  • Shareable invite links open a public web invite page that explains the League and sends people into the app. It never offers play.
  • After 24 hours without a match the empty state names who is missing ("2 more Women's singles players near your level would get you matches") with a Share button. Singles players also get a one-tap "Also search doubles".
  • While a doubles proposal waits for its last player, the others can share the open spot. The card shows format, day, venue and skill range, never names or photos.
  • The invite carries the inviter's code; the inviter is told when the friend joins, and for one week Circuit prefers pairing them if skill fits.
  • At most one share prompt per search and one invite push every 2 weeks.
  • People outside an open area can sign up, set a home area and see "7 players near you so far", invite friends and suggest venues. Circuit opens an area by hand once it has enough players and venues.
  • A "Circuit League plays here" QR poster is placed only with each venue's permission.
  • Founding Member and Recruiter badges reward early joiners and people who bring others in.
  • Ladder-member join path: existing organizer-ladder players get a direct route into the League, tracked as a sign-up source.

League Nights After launch

A recurring night at one venue, organized by a player or venue host and approved by Circuit. Players RSVP instead of searching, Circuit forms balanced rotating-partner foursomes, and every round is an ordinary rated League match with normal scoring and DUPR.

RoundsEach round is one rotating-doubles match (3 games to 11; 4 for Mixed). Report, confirm; top two move up a court, bottom two down.
  • The host is not an organizer: they record check-in and no-shows but cannot set results, ratings or eligibility, and never handle money through Circuit.
  • Players pay the venue directly for court time. No screen or record shows a court fee or payment.
  • The venue pitch: a free weekly league that fills off-peak courts with regulars who book and pay the venue directly. Indoor clubs often fill only 25–40% of off-peak hours; at Apex Cedar Park a non-member off-peak court is about $7.50 per player.
  • Hosts are any established League player or verified venue staff, and Circuit approves each night before it is listed. The host sets the category (or all three) and may set a DUPR range.
  • After the 24-hour lock, cancelling counts as a late cancellation. A host-reported no-show can be challenged, falls under the normal warning ladder, and repeat no-shows lose waitlist priority. If the host cancels for weather, it is never counted late.
  • Host rewards are non-monetary: a host badge and early RSVP. A venue may give hosts free court time on its own terms, outside Circuit. Circuit never pays hosts, takes commission, books courts or records court fees.
  • The page shows the host's first name and last initial, the window, Free or Paid, spots left and the RSVP count only once 5 players have RSVP'd. RSVP names are visible only to the night's players.
  • Venue partner page and League discounts with venues follow once a season of activity exists.

Behind the scenes

Operations, gating and launch health

Who runs what

  • Owner switches: a whole-League emergency gate, and a separate matching hold that stops new searches, matching and Match found delivery without stopping scoring, confirmation, disputes or DUPR sends. A hold never strands a played match.
  • Operators (authorized by a database table, not by screens): open areas, enter venues, approve League Nights, review flagged results, do recalibrations.
  • Launch week runs on database tools plus operator actions; a small phone-friendly /ops/league page follows.
  • At launch only the automatic-rules tier ships. The owner works everything else from one review queue, which also lets them hide a chat message, retry DUPR sends one at a time or in bulk and pause them during a DUPR outage, and receives Problem-with-this-result and Suggest-a-venue requests. Estimated effort without the agent: 8–15 hours a week in the first month, 5–9 after.
  • AI operations agent (after launch) works in four tiers: automatic rules, no AI (offer expiry, auto-confirm, reminders, DUPR retries, snapshots, the warning and 7-day pause); the agent decides with a recorded reason and an appeal (obvious typo corrections, hiding abusive chat, clear venue requests, support answers); the agent drafts, a person approves (reported photos, contested no-shows and 30-day pauses, rating corrections, every appeal); and a person only (permanent bans, threats, child-safety reports). It starts in shadow mode for 2–4 weeks, and a category goes autonomous once its proposals match the person's decisions at least 95% of the time. Estimated effort with the agent: 3–5 hours a week for 200–500 players. Case evidence goes to an AI provider that may not train on it, and the privacy policy says so.

Risks worth the product person's attention

Thin pools

Matching only works with enough searchers. 150 is the target; Women's singles, fixed Women's doubles and fixed Mixed will be thinnest. Mitigations are invites, Scan to play, venue widening and later League Nights.

Mobile-only handoff

Players without the app only reach the invite page, so install and sign-in recovery must be truthful and smooth. Marketing and video need an update to tell the consumer story alongside the organizer offering.

Texas age law

Reading the store's age signal applies to the whole app, not only the League. It ships with the League update and needs its own decision record.

Trust at scale

The owner personally works the review queue at launch. The flag-don't-hold approach trades some bad results counting briefly for speed and fewer stuck matches.
