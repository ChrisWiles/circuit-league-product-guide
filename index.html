Product guide for Circuit League

A pickleball league that organizes itself.

Circuit League is an automated matchmaking league inside the Circuit mobile app. A player says when they can play and where. Circuit finds opponents of the right level, proposes one concrete match, and handles scoring, ratings, leaderboards and DUPR reporting afterward. There is no organizer, no schedule to follow and no weekly commitment.

A product-review guide to the player experience, with illustrative screens and the decisions behind them.

Updated October 21, 2026 · Committed League changes through October 21, plus the approved scoring clarification awaiting implementation.

Product scope is not release status. The League work reviewed here is local implementation, not evidence of a production launch. The October 21 flexible-play rules below are approved but still need code changes and verification.

What changed since the October 9 guide?
  • Photos are optional. Players can join before they have venues or playing times.
  • Favorite venues are ranked, backups stay separate, and boards follow where matches were actually played.
  • Partnerships have no count limit or custom name; the two players' names identify the pair.
  • New match plans use games to 11. The latest approved policy lets players finish between completed games; implementation is pending.
  • Reported games have 48 hours for review, with a day-one push/email and a final push six hours before confirmation.
  • Boards have player search, genuine DUPR details and recent PD%. Weekly and season recaps open as manually paged stories.
  • Peer reviews, referral pairing boosts, the Recruiter badge, extra integrity signals and the automatic 30-day pause were removed.
  • Matching runs every minute. The web operator console is built; its matching hold preserves scoring and withdraws open offers fairly.

How to read the labels. Launch scope is intended for the Austin opening; it does not mean released. After launch is later work; unresolved designs are identified below. Deferred is postponed to a later season or phase. Not planned is explicitly out of scope.

Overview

What the League is, in one page

For players

Tell Circuit the times and venues that work. Get one proposed match at a time. Accept, tweak it or pass. Play, report the score, and watch your local position move.

For venues

More regulars playing at their courts. Later, League Nights fill off-peak hours. Players pay the venue directly; Circuit never touches court money.

For Circuit

A new player-facing product that runs on automation, with the owner reviewing a single queue. No revenue model at launch.

The big ideas

  1. Circuit picks the match. Players answer one proposal instead of browsing a list or negotiating in an open-ended chat.
  2. Standings come from results, not DUPR. Circuit keeps its own private rating, seeded once from DUPR. The number is never shown. Players see rank, movement and record.
  3. Actual games count. Eligible confirmed games are sent to DUPR individually. Unplayed and forfeited games have no score; stopped games have a separate eligibility rule.
  4. No fixed divisions or tiers. Matching uses rating closeness, with a fairness limit.
  5. Mobile only. League play lives in the mobile app. The website introduces the League and hosts a few supporting pages, never gameplay.
  6. Circuit never handles court money. No booking, no deposits, no fees collected.

Finding opponents

How matchmaking works

Matching runs every minute. It considers the longest-waiting search first and finds a legal group that shares time, allowed venues and a fair skill range. Favorites receive priority; a Backup can still be chosen when it works for everyone.

What a search is

  • Each format (singles, rotating doubles) has its own search, and each fixed team has its own. A player can run several at once.
  • A confirmed match stops only the search that found it, and drops the clashing times from the player's other searches.
  • There is no weekly cap on matches. A Tuesday match does not block a Sunday one.
  • Skip this week and Automatic weekly matchmaking control the weekly restart. Stopping never cancels a confirmed match.
  • Next week's times can be entered early. They are matched once inside the 7-day window.
  • If someone else cancels a confirmed match, the others go back into search automatically.

Fairness limit

Circuit only proposes matches inside a fairness limit, judged on the worst pairing in the match (for rotating doubles, the worst rotation). It relaxes the longer a search has waited, and never goes past 70/30 automatically.

Search ageWorst-pairing game-win split
First 24 hours60/40
24 – 72 hours65/35
After 72 hours70/30

There is no separate individual rating-gap limit for doubles. Fixed teams are judged on the two Team ratings, so an uneven pair such as 3.8 and 3.1 is matched on team strength. Automatic widening is on by default and a player can turn it off. The search clock starts when a search starts and resets only after a confirmed match from that search; editing times or declining does not reset it.

Matching avoids a plan whose scheduled end falls after sunset at a venue without lights. Venue access and an available court still need to be arranged by the players.

When pools are thin

A fair group still needs enough people who share a venue and time. No wait time or match rate is guaranteed. The 150-searcher launch target is a planning goal, not a promise; Women's singles, fixed Women's doubles and fixed Mixed are expected to be thinner pools.

Players can preview compatible times and privacy-protected popular hours before searching. After a long wait, the app offers relevant settings or sharing actions. A singles player can also start a doubles search. Widening needs approval; it never silently changes format, drops a fixed partner or removes the fairness limit. League Nights remain later work.

The core moment

Match found

When Circuit finds a group, everyone receives the same proposal at once: a time, a venue, a number of games and points. It is one decision, not a list to compare.

What a player can do

  • Accept. When everyone accepts the same plan, the match is confirmed. Before confirmation, an accepted player can change their answer without a conduct penalty.
  • Choose another time or venue. Up to 3 alternatives, some labelled "Outside your usual times". The player's own acceptance carries over to the plan they choose. The latest counter replaces any earlier one and does not extend the hold; after 3 rounds without agreement the proposal closes as "Not this time".
  • Not this time. Everyone returns to search. No penalty. Circuit does not re-offer the same group under unchanged conditions for 7 days.
  • Hide this group. Declines and hides that group for 7 days. After 3 hides in a row Circuit suggests reviewing search settings.
  • Chat. A match chat opens with the proposal for logistics ("where do we park?"). Agreeing a change in chat does not change the plan; someone has to send it as "Choose another time" and everyone must accept.

Match labels

The start is the best half-hour time inside everyone's saved windows, between 6 hours and 7 days ahead. The planned opening format is standard: 3 games to 11, or 4 for Mixed rotating doubles. Each proposal shows how even the match is: Close match Slight favourite Stretch match. No numbers are shown.

After everyone agrees

Confirmed match, play, and scoring

Before the match

  • Reminders: 24 hours before, only if confirmed more than 24 hours ahead, and 1 hour before. No response or Still on? step. The hour-before reminder never emails.
  • Calendar: add the current confirmed plan to a device calendar, Google Calendar or an ICS file. This is a one-time export; later cancellation does not automatically update that calendar.
  • Court access: every plan says Free or Paid. At a paid venue, Who's booking? lets a participant volunteer and release their own choice. Circuit never reserves a court, guarantees access or collects court money.
  • Check-in: optional and disclosed during setup. With consent and foreground permission, the phone can record a venue check-in near match time. Other players see the venue, never a live location. A check-in can lower review risk; it proves neither attendance nor play. Declining location still permits play.
  • Cancelling: players can cancel only before the scheduled start. Within 12 hours is late. Everyone is told who cancelled; the others' eligible searches return, while the canceller restarts deliberately. Weather uses cancellation, with at most a warning for rain. From the start, report scores or a no-show instead.
  • Venue changes: an agreed match retains its location details. Circuit can cancel a future match if a reviewed venue closes or moves materially; a spelling fix does not cancel it.

Game plans and actual play

Standard opening plan

New singles, fixed doubles and Men's/Women's rotating doubles plan 3 games to 11, win by 2. Mixed rotating doubles plans 4. Game-count and points pickers are removed. Historical matches keep their saved terms.

Players choose how much to play

The October 21 decision permits finishing between completed games or continuing after 2–0. Keep every actual game, including a third played game; do not force an injury label or invent a next score. Implementation pending.

Nothing played means no score

A forfeit or unplayed game has no credited 11–0. Pending and disputed games stay out of the rating and DUPR until they settle. A real stopped game keeps its actual points.

Approved policy, pending remediation. The committed October 19 implementation still requires planned games and complete added blocks unless a stop is recorded. The approved October 21 rule supersedes that requirement. The score-entry and closeout changes still need implementation and verification before the app can promise voluntary finishing.

Two different 48-hour clocks

  • Report: submit the played scores within 48 hours of the scheduled start. Without a report, the match ends No result, with no invented score or rating change.
  • Review: each reported game gets 48 elapsed hours from reporting. An eligible opponent can confirm immediately; silence confirms at the deadline.
  • At hour 24 of review: a reminder push and an email, if results email is enabled, ask the remaining responsible opponents to review.
  • At hour 42: a final push names the deadline and the automatic-confirmation/DUPR consequence. No second reminder email.
  • Confirmation or dispute stops that game's review reminders. The initial report and final reminder do not fall back to email.

Independent opposing confirmation

A reporter cannot confirm from their own account or device. In fixed doubles, the first eligible opposing answer settles the game. In rotating doubles, a player must have played against that game's reporter; a partner in one game may be an opponent in another. Each game's actual lineup is retained.

Missing-score reminders are separate: every player is asked to report 1 hour after the planned end, again at 24 hours, and 6 hours before the reporting deadline, by push and email.

Continuing and stopping early

The committed add-games action prepares a repeat of the full opening block: 3 games, or 4 for Mixed rotating doubles. Added games are to 11, under a ceiling of 9 extra games (therefore at most 8 for Mixed). A draft adds no score. Under the approved flexible-play rule, only the games actually played are retained even if the group does not finish that draft block; that admission change is pending.

An unfinished stopped game keeps the score when play stopped and records which side stopped. Completed games remain as played. The stopped game is sent to DUPR only if the continuing side led with at least 6 points; it is sent as a normal rated game, never a fabricated score.

October 21 optional-game exception · implementation pending. If a result was already settled and players continue, an injury in the optional game loses that game without reversing the earlier result. Example: A wins the first two games, then stops at 8–5 in the optional third. Keep 8–5 and the earlier result; A loses the stopped game. The stopped game is not DUPR-rated because the continuing side trails with 5.

Reading results and DUPR status

Reported and finished match screens lead with the score, review deadline and available response. Each game's winner appears first, teams stack vertically with their own scores, and winner green/stronger text and loser coral distinguish the outcome. Rotating results preserve each game's actual partners. Unresolved results retain their source order.

  • Eligible confirmed games go to DUPR individually as League matches. A report alone is not confirmation.
  • The app distinguishes sending, failed and synchronized results. It never invents a DUPR change or treats Circuit Rating movement as DUPR movement.
  • Each game revision has a bounded retry budget. A send that may already have reached DUPR is held for reconciliation rather than blindly sent twice. The owner can retry failures or pause sends.
  • Voided games are removed from DUPR. Renaming a venue never resends its games.
Fair matching and standings

The Circuit Rating (private)

The Circuit Rating is a hidden skill estimate. It decides who is matched with whom and orders the boards. Players never see the number.

What players see

Rank, movement ("Up 3"), record and a one-line reason for each change. Never the Circuit Rating number or its chart. Genuine, synced DUPR is shown separately.

How it starts

Seeded once from DUPR (with its reliability). No DUPR rating? Self-placement at 2.5 / 3.0 / "3.5 or higher". After that, only League results move it.

Three pools

Singles, Doubles and Teams are separate. A singles win never moves a doubles position. Category (Men's, Women's, Mixed) is a filter, not a pool.

How it moves

  • Games, not matches. Each game is a win or loss; margin of victory does not matter. All games in a match are weighted so a 5-game match does not count five times a 1-game match. The order of wins does not matter (W-W-L equals L-W-W).
  • Uncertainty. Each player has a confidence level. New players move fast; settled players move slowly. Returning after a long break speeds movement up a little.
  • Repeat opponents count less. Beating the same opponents again within 30 days earns less each time, which discourages farming wins with friends.
  • Teams. A new fixed pair starts from the average of the two partners' Doubles ratings with low confidence, then learns from its own results. A new partner means a new Team rating; old history stays with the old pair.
  • Ties in rank share the same position.
  • Reasons are stored. Every rating change is stored against the result that caused it, so Circuit can explain movement and repair corrected results.

Starting low on purposeNew players move fast, become board-eligible after 3 qualifying matches, and cannot win Season #1 until established. Placement is explained in their record; a lasting rating gap never silently replaces their Circuit Rating.

Flag, don't hold. Suspicious-looking results still count and still go to DUPR immediately. They join the owner's weekly review list only after 10 games together with strong combined signals. If the owner finds fake results, they are voided, ratings are replayed and games are deleted from DUPR.

Position and progress

Leaderboards, player records and recap stories

Earn a board by playing there

  • City → area → state → Everywhere. Eligibility follows the as-played venue confirmed with each game, not a declared home city, GPS or the original suggested court. A player who qualifies in Austin and Spicewood can appear on both boards.
  • For geographic boards, an established player needs 1 qualifying match in that place this season; a provisional player needs 3. Venue boards require at least 2 matches there, with the provisional threshold still applying. A fixed pair qualifies through its own results.
  • One private Circuit Rating per pool orders all qualifying places. A city has no separate local rating. Wins, win rate and PD% never determine rank.
  • A board needs at least 5 listed entries. Otherwise it explains the small pool and keeps the player's own season record available. Boards are member-only in the app.
  • Published boards refresh after Sunday closes in Central time. Each names its week or final season. Today's activity and placement progress stay separate from that frozen rank.
  • There is no home-city picker or 90-day city lock. Listed entries stay for that season; ties use competition ranks such as 1, 1, 1, 4.

Find a player without losing the board

Search covers every server page of the selected place, pool, category, season and snapshot. A result keeps its original rank. Place and format remain visible; category and season choices sit in a draft Filters sheet and apply together. The season picker appears only when there is a choice. Scrolling loads more players and preserves a pinned own position.

Names and real DUPR, separate from rank

Rows show rank, privacy-approved names, match record, movement and recent PD%, without photos, initials or a Provisional badge. A pair uses one name per line, alphabetically. Tap a name to open the record, where placement status remains available.

The inline rating is genuine synced DUPR: singles on Singles, doubles on Doubles, and each partner's own doubles rating on Teams. Tap it for format, reliability and freshness context. NR means linked but unrated. Former/deleted or unlinked identities expose no rating. Even a historical League board shows the latest synced DUPR, clearly identified as current; the historical League rank remains frozen.

PD% explains recent form, not rank

PD% is points scored minus points conceded, divided by their sum, multiplied by 100. Recent form uses up to 10 confirmed, fully played games in the selected season, pool and category, with the actual sample size. Individual Doubles includes fixed and rotating play; each pair has its own sample. Changing geography changes rank, not this sample.

Player and pair records

  • Current/best position, rank history and movement within the selected board, pool and category.
  • Season games won/lost and season-total PD%, recent form with its game count, and separate individual/pair history.
  • Actual partners, opponents, scores and result state. Other players' histories use week buckets; exact played dates and venues remain in the player's own history.
  • Founding Member, Season complete and Season #1 badges open explanations tied to the actual season and board.

Achievement pills

Confirmed results can show achievements such as 11–0 pickle, Comeback win, Clutch, Revenge and Took a game off the #1. The Upset win pill is removed. Week achievements include Perfect games, Undefeated, Win streak, Up 3 and Season high. Rank achievements use published board history; a first listing or leaving provisional placement is not movement.

Pills celebrate the subject, have at most 5 per result row, avoid opponent names in share cards and never send a standalone push or email. Pending, disputed, voided or forfeited results receive none; corrections recompute affected achievements.

Weekly and season stories

Recap links open a manually paged story in the native app, with Previous, Next, Replay and a readable View summary route. There is no autoplay. Reduce Motion retains the same facts; the summary keeps period selection, standings links and weekly matchmaking actions.

Stories use the saved recipient recap. A past week never borrows current standings, and a season recap never invents a starting rank. No play produces a short factual story. Rotating-doubles results retain each game's own two sides rather than assigning later scores to the first pairing.

Weekly delivery is due Monday at 8 AM local time and follows recap preferences and the optional-push cap. Weekly and season email link View recap to that saved period; weekly email retains Start matchmaking as a secondary action. Board movements group locations by format/category.

Competition facts stay frozen. Account erasure removes the deleted person's name from future recap reads and deliveries, replacing it with Former player; already delivered messages cannot be recalled.

Partners and places

My Teams and the venue directory

Persistent fixed partnerships

  • Invite a partner by search or private link, review the category and partner identity, then accept. There is no custom team-name field: the two privacy-approved names identify the pair, alphabetically.
  • There is no limit on the number of partnerships or pending invitations. Each exact pair has its own search and rating, constrained by both partners' times and existing commitments.
  • Shared setup intersects preferences without exposing either full calendar. Either partner can accept within both players' saved times; otherwise both must agree.
  • Retiring and re-forming the same pair preserves its rating and history. A different partner creates a new pair; it does not inherit the former pair's record.
  • A partner can pause searching or mark themselves Out injured without ending the pair. Circuit never substitutes a partner automatically.
  • A ban, account deletion or partner block ends the pair and cancels future matches together. Other participants receive no penalty. A partner block can still invoke the ordinary late-cancellation rule unless covered by a qualifying Report.

Favorites and backups

Saved venues are separate from discovery results. Favorites have a numbered order; Backups are allowed without extra priority. Players can reorder Favorites and save the order. Circuit first prefers a venue favored by more of the group, then uses favorite order to break the tie. A venue everyone allows is still required; no ranking expands that allowed set.

Choose venues by city search or current location across opened areas. Distance, Free/Paid access and reviewed notes help players decide. A listing guarantees neither an open court nor membership access. Paid access is arranged with the venue, outside Circuit.

Suggest missing courts and track the review

A player can send a venue name, city/state and address or public map/venue link, with optional court, lighting and access hints. Circuit reviews the location, duplicates, access and area before publishing a trusted listing. A map pin is selected on the review map; suggested details do not become facts without review.

The player's private history distinguishes In review, Available, Listed in an unopened area, Unavailable and Not added, with a public reason when supplied. Operators' internal notes stay private. Becoming available never saves the venue, picks a time or starts a search for the player. Any availability notice follows the existing announcement preference and push cap.

Popular hours without revealing schedules

Venue details use that reviewed venue's local time and area. Popular-time cells require at least five distinct eligible contributors and never reveal an individual's availability. Empty data is not a forecast of open courts. Players can use the aggregate view when choosing their own times; Circuit does not invent popularity or availability.

Trust and safety

Blocks, reports and conduct decisions

Explicit reports, not peer ratings

Optional post-match thumbs, themes and comments were removed. A score dispute or a declined offer is not misconduct. Circuit acts on recorded cancellations, explicit reports and reviewed evidence; there is no public conduct score.

Mute, block and report

Mute hides messages without telling the other player. Block prevents future League matching, up to 5 active blocks, without changing standings. Blocking an opponent does not cancel an agreed match; blocking a fixed partner ends that pair, with the consequence shown first. Report sends a case and an immediate owner alert.

One warning, then a matching pause

Late cancellations are recorded when made. A no-show must be reported by an original opponent within its reporting window, never inferred from a missing score or check-in. The player has 48 hours to challenge. The first substantiated incident is a warning; 3 in 60 days cause a 7-day pause of new matching. There is no automatic 30-day tier.

Review and support

A challenged case goes to the owner. Restrictions explain the reason, affected capability and support email, with no promised response deadline. Existing matches, scoring, chat and reporting remain available during a matching pause. Only a reasoned owner decision can impose a permanent League ban.

A confirmed game outweighs a no-show

One match/player produces at most one incident, however many people report. A confirmed game prevents a no-show finding against a player recorded in it. A no-show report involving a pending game disputes that game; an unagreed game can be removed while the conduct case follows its own challenge clock. Being named in a draft, a GPS check-in or a status change never proves play.

Integrity review

The private weekly review keeps one fake-result pattern: a sufficiently large set of repeated-opponent games with a strong combination of lopsided results, timeout confirmation, empty chat and documented alternatives. At least 10 finished games and the combined thresholds are required. Blocks, ordinary disputes or declining a stretch offer do not create separate integrity signals. Check-in evidence can lower risk but never establishes that a game happened.

Flagged confirmed results still count and eligible games go to DUPR. A flag is triage, not a finding. If the owner identifies fake games, those games are voided, ratings replay and their DUPR submissions are deleted.

Photos, chat and account deletion

  • Photos are optional and platform-wide. Upload screening and global reporting apply wherever Circuit shows a player. Held or reported photos fall back to initials; a hidden photo does not revoke League membership.
  • Chat is for logistics. Mute, Block and Report remain available. Operators do not hide individual messages. A conversation closes 7 days after its result or cancellation and is retained privately for 180 days for reports/disputes.
  • Deletion requests are accepted. Specified pending reviews can delay execution for at most 14 days; League does not refuse the request. The profile and photo are removed and surviving history shows Former player. Upcoming matches cancel without penalty to others and fixed pairs end. Deletion never prevents a later result void or required DUPR deletion.
  • DUPR history is separate. Account deletion alone does not recall results already sent there; the player uses DUPR's deletion process.
  • Entry policy: published category rules, separate release/terms acceptance and the existing reporting/support path apply. The join-time terms remain; automatic legal renewal and an arbitration opt-out process are not part of the current design.

Privacy promises

  • A proposal shows its shared plan and alternatives, never someone else's calendar. Venue boards show who qualifies there, never when they will play.
  • No precise home location, raw availability, block/report details or private Circuit Rating is exposed. Genuine synced DUPR is separately labeled.
  • Names default to first name and last initial unless the player chooses full-name visibility. Alphabetical pairing labels respect that choice.
  • No continuous tracking, live 0–0, inferred playing now or promise that Circuit knows who reached the court.
  • Analytics and error reports exclude private names, contact details, raw scores, ratings, message text and coordinates.

Match found, counter-proposal, unanswered, search-off and proposal-progress notices wait from 10 PM to 7 AM venue-local time. The offer's answer clock counts awake hours. Other notices follow their own delivery rules; players can also use phone Do Not Disturb.

Caps protect optional notices

Getting enough players

Scan to play and invitations

One permanent personal code

The same code powers Scan, the invite link and the printable flyer. It works until its owner chooses Reset my code; it does not need to remain open on the owner's screen. Resetting invalidates old copies for Scan/new referrals but preserves earlier attribution.

Scan starts a plan; each player consents

  1. The host selects a reviewed venue and format, then scans the other players' codes.
  2. Every player reviews the same plan and confirms on their own installed app. The host sees whether everyone is ready.
  3. Once anyone confirms, the plan is locked; a change needs a new Scan session.
  4. Only the host's Start publishes the confirmed match. A scan or invitation alone does not enroll someone in play.

A newcomer can scan a member's code with their camera, install the app and complete light sign-up with age, category, DUPR and legal checks. No place is held during installation. A photo is optional. The player finishes venue/time setup after the first confirmed game.

Scan requires the actual group's consent and eligibility. A large skill gap can be explicitly accepted after a warning; Scan is not an instant-play queue. A rotating group can replace a missing player before play under the existing replacement flow; it cannot rewrite players who already played. Someone didn't come instead ends a confirmed match as not played, creates no score and lets present players report the named absences.

Invite without changing competitive rules

  • Personal links open the website's League explanation and app handoff. Installation, sign-in and eligibility still apply.
  • An accepted doubles player waiting on the last spot can share prefilled plan text and a normal invite link. It includes no player names, photos or ratings, and grants no automatic place.
  • The inviter is told when a friend joins. There is no referral pairing preference, Recruiter badge or inviter reward counter.
  • The first confirmed game with the sign-up source remains an internal referral-funnel fact; it does not award the inviter anything.
  • Automatic share prompts are limited to once per search episode after a 24-hour wait, and invite pushes to once per 14 days. Manual sharing remains available.
  • Members without local courts can invite friends and suggest venues. No declared city, nearby-member count or notify-me list is required, and no search begins until reviewed venues and times exist.
  • Flyers use the player's own code. Venue posters require permission. Existing organizer-ladder players have a tracked join route into League.

League Nights After launch

Later concept, not built for launch. Details below remain planning material and need a fresh design/release decision.

A recurring night at one venue, organized by a player or venue host and approved by Circuit. Players RSVP instead of searching, Circuit forms balanced rotating-partner foursomes, and every round is an ordinary rated League match with normal scoring and DUPR.

  • The host is not an organizer: they record check-in and no-shows but cannot set results, ratings or eligibility, and never handle money through Circuit.
  • Players pay the venue directly for court time. No screen or record shows a court fee or payment.
  • The venue pitch: a free weekly league that fills off-peak courts with regulars who book and pay the venue directly. The proposed benefit is more regular off-peak bookings; this guide makes no current claim about a venue's prices or occupancy.
  • Hosts are any established League player or verified venue staff, and Circuit approves each night before it is listed. The host sets the category (or all three) and may set a DUPR range.
  • After the 24-hour lock, cancelling counts as a late cancellation. A host-reported no-show can be challenged, falls under the normal warning ladder, and repeat no-shows lose waitlist priority. If the host cancels for weather, it is never counted late.
  • Host rewards are non-monetary: a host badge and early RSVP. A venue may give hosts free court time on its own terms, outside Circuit. Circuit never pays hosts, takes commission, books courts or records court fees.
  • The page shows the host's first name and last initial, the window, Free or Paid, spots left and the RSVP count only once 5 players have RSVP'd. RSVP names are visible only to the night's players.
  • Venue partner page and League discounts with venues follow once a season of activity exists.

Behind the scenes

Operations and launch health

The owner works the web console

The built operator console has Queue, Health and Controls, plus tools for matches, DUPR failures, badges, pauses, bans and announcements. Cases include result problems, safety/conduct, integrity and venue suggestions. Authorized decisions record who acted, when and why. There is no native operator console.

  • Opening areas: Circuit reviews the venue directory and opens an area deliberately. There is no separate whole-League feature switch.
  • Matching hold: stops new searches, matching and Match found delivery, and withdraws open offers without a miss or penalty. Scoring, confirmation, disputes and existing match work continue.
  • DUPR send pause: stops new sends during an outage while already claimed sends finish. It is separate from the matching hold.
  • Human decisions: the owner reviews challenged conduct, reports, fake-result cases, result corrections and bans. Automatic rules handle routine clocks and delivery. The old autonomous-agent authority tiers are removed, as is operator hiding of individual chat messages.

What runs automatically

Every minute

Fairness and availability checks, ordinary matching and eligible fill-ins. A changed search can be considered on the next run.

At the due time

Offer expiry, 48-hour score confirmation, review/report reminders, 24-hour dispute resolution, chat closure and eligible DUPR delivery/retries.

Weekly

Sunday snapshots, Monday recap delivery and the private fake-result review list. Recaps preserve their saved competition facts.

The four health alerts cover matching stale for 30 minutes, a reported game still unconfirmed after 48 hours and 30 minutes, exhausted DUPR retries, and an intimate-image removal approaching its deadline. Job failures remain visible in error monitoring. A health screen is not proof that production scheduling and delivery credentials are enabled.
